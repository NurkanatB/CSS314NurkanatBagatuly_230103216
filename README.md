# CS482 Practicum: Memory Hierarchy, Contention and Scaling

**Bottleneck findings.** Host: Ryzen 5 4600H (6 cores / 12 threads, 32 KB L1D and 512 KB L2 per core, 8 MB shared L3, 64 B lines) with a single 8 GB DDR4-3200 module (25.6 GB/s peak); salted N = 1344. Strided reads cost 1.9-2.9 ns/element at strides 1-4 and 4.7-7.1 ns at stride >= 8 (about 2-3x, noisy), and the interchanged i-k-j loop was 6.5x faster than i-j-k (0.061 s vs 0.397 s, M = 672, Numba, bit-identical results). For false sharing, with workers pinned to separate physical cores, unpadded counters took 1.62 s at P = 4 versus 0.92 s at P = 1 (S = 0.57), while padded stayed flat (0.91 s) and privatized was fastest (0.19 s); P = 2 showed no penalty, and at P = 8 every variant slows because 8 workers exceed the 6 physical cores (SMT). Karp-Flatt e = (P*T_P/T_1 - 1)/(P - 1) rises 1.03 -> 2.02 -> 2.77 for unpadded but stays at 0.98 for padded at P = 2 and 4; since each worker does fixed work, ideal e is 1 by this formula, and normalising for total work gives e = 0.25 (unpadded) versus about 0 (padded) at P = 4. The stencil is memory-bandwidth bound: strong-scaling speedup plateaus at 1.63x from P = 4 (efficiency 27% at P = 6; Amdahl s = 0.47, S_max = 2.14, which mostly reflects bandwidth saturation rather than serial code), weak-scaling throughput reaches only 1.59x at P = 6, and the arithmetic intensity of 0.31 FLOP/byte (5 FLOPs per 16 DRAM bytes) lies far below the ridge point of 11.25 FLOP/byte (288 GFLOP/s peak, 25.6 GB/s).
